Taoist Lovemaking

Classical NameFang chung shu — the arts of the bedchamber
PrincipleConserve jing, transform it, circulate it
Related ToShih Shui — bone marrow washing
Open ToAdult men and women, single or partnered

Taoist tradition treats sexual energy as one of the body's three treasures rather than as something separate from health or spiritual practice. The classical texts call it jing — essence — and they are consistent on one point: it is finite, it is spent carelessly by most people, and it can instead be conserved, transformed, and circulated to strengthen the whole system.

This is old material. The arts of the bedchamber, fang chung shu, appear in Chinese medical writing alongside herbalism and acupuncture and were treated with the same seriousness. They were not a curiosity at the margins of Taoist practice. They were part of it.

The Three Treasures

The framework this sits inside is simple to state and considerably harder to embody:

  • Jing — essence, the densest of the three, closely associated with sexual and reproductive vitality
  • Chi — energy, what jing becomes when it is refined rather than expended
  • Shen — spirit, what chi becomes at the highest levels of cultivation

The work is that refinement upward. Methods are taught for conserving jing rather than depleting it, transforming what is conserved into usable chi, and directing that chi along the same pathways the meditation and breathing practices already use.

The aim is not restraint for its own sake. Ascetic denial belongs to a different tradition. This one holds that the energy is meant to be cultivated — neither discarded nor suppressed.

Relationship to Shih Shui

This material overlaps directly with Shih Shui, where work with sexual energy is central rather than incidental. Students who come to one are generally taught the other alongside it, because separating them makes both harder to understand.

What Is Taught

Breathing methods, energy circulation, conservation practice, and the traditional health framework that explains why any of it matters. Instruction is technical and follows the classical material rather than modern popularisations of it.

Both men and women study this, partnered or not. It is taught privately, to adults, and to students who approach it as cultivation rather than novelty.
