Hsing-I Chuan

CharacterDirect, linear, committed
CoreFive elements · Twelve animals
PrinciplesSix harmonies · Nine essences
Lineage HereThrough Master Chiao and Master Hsu Hong-Chi

The name means something close to "form and intention boxing," and the order matters: the intention comes first and the body follows it. Hsing-I is the most direct of the internal arts. Where Ba Kua circles and Tai-Chi yields, Hsing-I tends to move straight through — one decision, whole body, no second guess.

It is often described as easy to learn and hardest to master, and that reputation is deserved. The outer shapes are few and simple enough to show a beginner in an afternoon. Making them actually work — with the whole body arriving at once, rooted, without a gap between the thought and the step — is the work of decades.

The Five Elements

Five fists form the alphabet of the system, each associated with an element and an organ, and each capable of being trained as a health exercise or a fighting method depending on how it is drilled:

  • Pi Chuan — splitting, associated with metal
  • Tsuan Chuan — drilling, associated with water
  • Beng Chuan — crushing, associated with wood
  • Pao Chuan — pounding, associated with fire
  • Heng Chuan — crossing, associated with earth

These are trained not as five techniques but as five ways of organising the entire body. They generate and destroy one another in sequence, which is how they combine into linked forms and two-person work.

The Twelve Animals

Beyond the elements sit twelve animal methods — dragon, tiger, monkey, horse, water lizard, chicken, hawk, swallow, snake, tai bird, eagle, bear. Each expresses a different strategy and a different quality of movement, and each corrects something the elements alone will not.

Underneath all of it are the six harmonies and the nine essences — the structural requirements that separate Hsing-I from arm-punching that happens to use its shapes.

Health and Fighting

Hsing-I has always been taught as both. The same five fists that train striking power also drive circulation through the organ systems they correspond to, which is why the art was historically as popular with the aging as with bodyguards. Master Hsu Hong-Chi, one of the teachers in our lineage, was known as much for his healing work as for his fighting.
