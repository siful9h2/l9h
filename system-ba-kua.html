Ba Kua Chang

Core PracticeWalking the circle
StructureEight palms · Eight animals · Ten exercises
WalkingThree distinct styles of step
Lineage HereThrough Master Chiao Chang-Hung

Ba Kua takes its name and its structure from the eight trigrams of the I-Ching, and it behaves the way that suggests: everything in it is change. Where Hsing-I commits straight forward and Tai-Chi absorbs and returns, Ba Kua simply is not where the attack lands. It moves around, and it keeps moving.

The signature practice is walking the circle — a continuous, spiralling step around a fixed centre while the upper body holds and changes palm positions. It looks like a meditation and functions as one, but it is also the entire fighting method in compressed form. Everything else in the system is an elaboration of what the circle teaches.

Why Circle Walking

  • It builds a base that stays under you while the body twists — the physical prerequisite for everything else
  • It trains the habit of taking angles rather than meeting force head-on
  • It develops the continuous rotational power the palm changes express
  • It is stamina work, structure work, and meditation at once, which is why it is never outgrown
A practitioner who has walked the circle for twenty years is still walking the circle. That is not a failure to progress. That is the art.

The Curriculum

Onto the walking are built the eight palm changes, eight animal expressions, ten supporting exercises, and three distinct styles of walking, each with its own timing and application. The system also has an extensive weapons component, though that comes late.

The Goal

What Ba Kua is ultimately training is not a set of responses but their absence — the capacity to move, act, and react with total natural freedom, without a plan and without hesitation. The eight changes are scaffolding. The point is what remains when the scaffolding comes down.
