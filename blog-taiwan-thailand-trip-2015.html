Training in Taiwan and Thailand, 2015

AuthorSifu James W. McNeil
First publishedL9H Internal Kung-Fu newsletter, December 2015 (Volume 8, Issue 12)
SeriesTravel and training

Adapted for the web from Sifu James W. McNeil's newsletter, lightly edited. Sifu McNeil is the Gate Keeper of the Little Nine Heaven Wu Tao System and the teacher of Sifu Ajay Kumra, who teaches privately in Santee, California.

Taipei

In October 2015, three of my students met in Taipei, Taiwan, where my niece, a Taiwanese national, welcomed them with true Taiwanese hospitality in the Da-an district. The streets and parks fill up early in the morning with people young and old exercising, and every day in Taiwan my students were up and outside by six a.m. training.

The days were filled with joy, love and culture. They visited the Chiang Kai-shek Memorial, Chin Nan Temple, the Huang Ti Temple, natural hot springs, and the many night markets, museums and temples of Taipei. The evenings were full of street games, karaoke and, of course, dinner at the famous Snake Alley.

Bangkok

We had planned this seminar for a while. Only a few people understand the importance of feeling in modern life and the joy that goes with it, so we had to choose the right place: good weather, friendly people, and somewhere to train without interruption. Once again the choice was Thailand, the land of a million smiles.

Eight of us met in Bangkok on October 29th and trained on the roof of our hotel, in a private garden overlooking the city skyline, for four or more hours each morning. After training we swam and had a massage, then ate dinner together and trained for another two hours in the evening. Many nights we gathered to tell stories and talk about kung-fu.

One evening, as we discussed the importance of feeling, a bright green light filled the entire room. We went to the balcony and saw an amazingly massive meteor cross the night sky. We later learned it was seen all across Thailand.

The Training

Each day the students trained and made corrections to perfect their forms: Chen Tai-Chi first and second forms, Hsing-I, Ba Kua, Splashing Hands and Little Nine Heaven Wu Tao, each system on a different day. Everyone practiced very hard to cover all the material. In their free time some students visited temples, museums, parks and markets, and others took classes in traditional Thai massage.

Four students drove two hours outside Bangkok to a small Buddhist village to receive a sak yant, a traditional prayer tattoo, from an elderly monk who said a prayer before and after each one. They were not allowed to pay him, but offered a small token of incense and cut flowers for the temple.

Everyone trained very hard and felt they had learned a lot, and it was a happy time with no problems. We hope to plan another trip next year.
