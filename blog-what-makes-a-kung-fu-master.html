What Makes a Kung Fu Master? Not the Title

AuthorSifu James W. McNeil
First publishedL9H Internal Kung-Fu newsletter, May 2018 (Volume 11, Issue 5)

Adapted for the web from Sifu James W. McNeil's newsletter, lightly edited. Sifu McNeil is the Gate Keeper of the Little Nine Heaven Wu Tao System and the teacher of Sifu Ajay Kumra, who teaches privately in Santee, California.

The word "Master" is very misused in today's world. What makes a master of any sport is hard work, dedication and a lifetime of learning and training. I know a few kung fu and karate teachers who call themselves "Master," "Grandmaster" or even "Great Grandmaster," and they don't feel they need to practice what they teach anymore because they are "Masters." We even have black belts who call themselves "Masters." A black belt means two things: you have something to hold your pants up, or you are now ready to really begin learning.

I know of a few "Masters" who have gone to many different kung fu schools and learned a little of each style. They created their own system from the bits and pieces they "learned" and now consider themselves a master of the system they invented. Some go to China and have a picture taken with a true Master, or maybe study for a month or so, and return to their country as a black belt or even a "Master" of kung fu or karate.

"Kung fu" roughly translates to hard work diligently done. This does not mean you are diligent for a year or two and then you are a Master. It means diligence over a lifetime of training, and a love of what you are doing for the pure joy of doing it, not for accolades, adoration or money.

There is also the marketing "Master," who writes books, makes videos and has a great marketing team to promote him as a "Master" of chi kung, kung fu, meditation and so on. They usually can't even show you a picture of the teachers they claim to have learned from, most likely because they never had one.

How My Masters Trained

I practice at least four to six hours a day, five days a week when I am home, not counting two hours of meditation. I do not consider myself a "Master," even though two of my Masters have handed down their systems to me. I practice because I feel I still have much to learn, and I keep practicing to perfect what my Masters taught me. In the last 50 years, I have never been without a teacher for more than five weeks, and I have not missed more than a total of six months of training. I still practice daily, and my body is stronger and healthier.

My teacher, Master Pan Wing-Chow, practiced Chen Tai-Chi every morning until his passing. Grandmaster Chiao Chang-Hung practiced Ba-Kua, Tai-Chi, Little Nine Heaven Wu Tao and meditation every day, and he slept only two hours a day until he journeyed on at 87. Master Chin Cheng-Yen practiced Tzu Men Chuan and Tai-Chi until his passing. These were Masters who continued their training their entire lives so they could perfect their systems and make their own Masters proud.

In past times, one was either a student or a Master. I know of a few students who left their home country as students and arrived in another country with the title of Master. It is amazing how many times this happens. They introduce themselves as a Master and are usually believed without having to show any knowledge or skill.

A Form Is Only a Form

Anyone can do a form and make it look beautiful or powerful. But when doing that form, do you understand and feel every part of your body's movements? If not, then it is just a meaningless form. Do you know the fighting aspect of each and every move? If not, it is only a form. Do you understand the chi kung aspect of the form? If not, it is only a form. When working with weapons, do you understand the fighting aspect of each little move? If not, it is only a useless form. Do you feel and understand the reeling-silk aspect of the form in every movement? If not, it is only a form. A true Master understands all these things and more, because it is knowledge that must be learned in order to evolve the form to perfection.

It is sad that most of the old true Masters have passed away. They were Masters because they practiced and tried to perfect their systems. They would say they learned something every time they practiced or taught their art to a student. Their learning never stopped. They also realized that they had to master the basics before they could effectively teach others.

There are people who have trained for five years, stop training for 15 to 20 years, and come back saying they have 20 to 25 years' experience and claim to be a Master. Closely related is the student who barely grasps the basics and tries to teach what little they know, which is far from perfected.

Keeping the Old Ways

Chinese kung fu has been around for thousands of years. The only bad thing is that the old ways are being thrown aside and new modern kung fu is replacing the traditional ways. The so-called "new style" Chen Tai-Chi falls into this category, as do some Ba Kua and Hsing-I systems. Many even shorten the form from 64 to 24 moves. I do not teach Wu Shu or the new styles; I teach the old traditional styles of kung fu. The Little Nine Heaven Wu Tao system has never been changed and is still pure today.

I respect all the martial artists who have kept the traditional martial arts alive by practicing and teaching for many years without altering the styles. I am only against the words "Master" and "Grandmaster" for those who don't deserve them. True Masters seem to be very few and far between these days.

The question many people ask me is why I have trained every day for the past 50 years. One reason is that I love kung fu, and I enjoy training and learning. I am still trying to feel and perfect what I have learned. I want my Masters to be proud of me, and I promised them I would train daily. I enjoy teaching my students in the hope that they will continue in my Masters' footsteps and mine, to keep and preserve the old ways of kung fu.

My Training Schedule

I follow this schedule five days a week:

  • Meditation: one hour in the morning and one hour in the evening
  • Shih Shui Kung: one hour in the morning
  • Standing meditation: 30 minutes
  • Sit-ups, push-ups, pull-ups, jogging, bicycling and ab rollers: two hours
  • Complete Iron Hand training: one hour
  • Chen Tai-Chi first and second forms, Ba Kua and Little Nine Heaven kung fu: two hours

No, I do not consider myself a Master. I only want my Masters to be proud of me, as I promised them I would always continue to practice hard. A name is only a name; it doesn't make your kung fu good.
