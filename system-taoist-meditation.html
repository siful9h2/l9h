The Systems · Foundation

Taoist Meditation

FormsSitting and standing
FocusBreath regulation and stillness
FunctionFoundation beneath every other system
RequirementConsistency, not duration

Every art on this site rests on this one. The internal systems are called internal because of what happens in the practitioner's attention, not because of how the movements look, and meditation is where that attention is built.

Two forms are taught. Seated practice works with breath, posture, and the gradual settling of mental activity. Standing practice — holding a fixed posture for extended periods — does something the seated work cannot: it trains stillness while the body is under load, which is the condition the martial arts actually operate in.

Standing Practice

Standing is where most students meet the real difficulty. Nothing happens. The legs burn, the mind produces every possible reason to stop, and the only instruction is to remain. What that trains is not endurance but the capacity to stay present in discomfort without reacting to it — which turns out to be the same skill the fighting arts need most.

The internal arts do not become internal through years of practice. They become internal at the moment attention stops wandering. Meditation is how that moment is trained.

What It Is Not

This is not a relaxation programme, and it is not tied to any religion you would have to adopt. It is a structured technical practice with a specific aim: stillness deep enough that chi can be felt, directed, and eventually elevated. Students of any belief or none practise it without conflict.

Getting Started

Ten consistent minutes a day will take a student further than an occasional hour. We start people short and lengthen the sessions only when the shorter ones have become genuinely stable.
